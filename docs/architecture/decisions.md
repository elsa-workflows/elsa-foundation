# Kernel decision register

> **Approved design, with conditions.** Gate 2 granted design approval on 18 November 2026 at
> `addffea7c850063d1ebe710b07866ff1640a93cb` ([verdict](https://github.com/elsa-workflows/elsa-foundation/issues/4229#issuecomment-6990560103)); the implementation evidence is owed at K1 to K12, and the
> [gate 2 conditions](../program-goals/clean-slate-kernel.md#gate-2-conditions) stay open until it lands. This register lists every owner decision that binds the clean-slate
> kernel's design, grouped by topic, with its date, a one-line rule and its source. The pages hold the detail; the
> proposal ADR links here. A decision changes only by a new owner decision, recorded here with its date.

Decisions are by Sipke Schoorstra, the owner, for [program #4229](https://github.com/elsa-workflows/elsa-foundation/issues/4229).
"Sketch" is the [design sketch, draft 5](../reports/clean-slate-kernel/design-sketch-draft-5.md). A source marked
"owner card" was given in the coordinator's session and has no earlier public record; this register is its first.

## The gate 2 fourth-review round (18 November)

Taken while answering the [fourth gate 2 architecture review](https://github.com/elsa-workflows/elsa-foundation/issues/4229#issuecomment-6967291576). No owner decision was needed: every rule below preserves the owner's cards (qualified deletes, tenant isolation, recovery in place, the case-insensitive collation pin) and is the coordinator's design choice.

| Date | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 18 Nov | Release cohort precedence: rows a recover drain released (`ReleasedBy` of the creation's latest generation, not `Done`) keep delivery precedence over every non-control row of the instance, during the drain and after its Complete, until consumed; control kinds come first only while the drain is open, and after Complete a control waits behind the non-held, offerable cohort rows and then takes its built place. Complete sets a durable `Cohort (creation, generation)` mark on the instance row whenever a row it released may still be unconsumed. While the mark is set, the owner offers no non-cohort row of the instance, control kinds included, until a paged read (at most `P_page` rows per read, skipping ticket kinds and the cohort keys the model holds) finds no offerable, non-held cohort row; the next row is then offered with the held rows handed back. The mark itself is cleared only when a read finds no cohort row at all, held or not, by a fenced write that names the `(c, g)` it read. Ticket kinds (job, meet and publish tickets) never count as cohort rows; a released job result does, while a running job's result arrives as a new, untagged row. Cohort suspects are tried first, a suspect the cohort rule passes over or the open drain withholds is shelved so a bounded poll moves on, and while a recover drain is open or the mark is set every cohort row is exempt from a control's recovery cut, in trials and in ordinary delivery (fourth review R4-B1). | Coordinator design choice, not an owner card; [observability](observability.md#resolving-a-park-a-bounded-drain) |
| 18 Nov | After a page commit lands, the owner refreshes from the committed values every row the commit retagged that it holds in memory (held, on trial, or polled and not yet delivered), and discards those keys from a poll that read pre-commit versions; Hold and trial semantics and the update count are unchanged (fourth review R4-M4). | Coordinator design choice, not an owner card; [observability](observability.md#resolving-a-park-a-bounded-drain) |
| 18 Nov | A retirement marker with a recorded parent keeps a bounded cancellation obligation: `Parent`, `ParentCreation`, and a layer-2-neutral list the actor type hands over at retirement, its children as `InstanceRef` and creation and its jobs as job key and `JobGeneration`. ## The node lease amendment (19 November)

The owner's card of 19 November replaced the per-partition lease renewal with a node-level heartbeat (above). The rules
below carry it out; each is a coordinator design choice under that card. The independent architecture review (run on
Claude Fable at the owner's choice, Codex being out of credits) returned CHANGES REQUIRED on `a73557f4e`
([first pass](https://github.com/elsa-workflows/elsa-foundation/issues/4229#issuecomment-7005856502)) and APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS on `170ac1f7c` ([delta pass](https://github.com/elsa-workflows/elsa-foundation/issues/4229#issuecomment-7006654424)); its four delta
Minors are corrected here, and its condition NL-C1 is tracked in the plan's
[gate 2 conditions](../program-goals/clean-slate-kernel.md#gate-2-conditions). [Ownership and fencing](ownership-and-fencing.md) and the
[storage model](storage-model.md) state them in place, and K1b builds them.

| Date | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 19 Nov | The lease moves to one node record per process, `Nodes (Id, SeenAt, LeaseUntil)` in database time. A partition row keeps `Owner`, `Epoch`, `Position` and `Died`, and no lease. `Owner` and `Nodes.Id` are the process name, unique per process start (finding F9), so a restarted process is a new owner and its predecessor's partitions expire with the predecessor's node record. | Coordinator design choice under the owner card; [ownership and fencing](ownership-and-fencing.md#the-partition-record) |
| 19 Nov | A take accepts a free partition, a partition whose owner's node record is missing or has `LeaseUntil` before database time, or a retake by the recorded owner. It reads the owners' node records in its transaction without locking them, increments each taken row's epoch, and renews the taker's own node record in the same transaction. Safety rests on the epoch, as before; a renewal that commits after the take's read keeps only the partitions the take left alone. | Coordinator design choice; [ownership and fencing](ownership-and-fencing.md#the-partition-record) |
| 19 Nov | Deaths count as before: only a take from another process whose node lease has expired, or whose node record is missing, increments `Attempts` on the previous epoch's unfinished trial messages. The take decides by its plain read, so a renewal that races it can cost one death count on the partitions it moved; the fence protects correctness. | Coordinator design choice; [storage model](storage-model.md#message-transition-details) |
| 19 Nov | Every host of a Worker owns its node's heartbeat (the built-in runtime's node loop, the fixed host, the simulation's node), on its own cadence and never behind a balancing round's moves; it hands the Worker the answer and the monotonic send time. The heartbeat is one statement or transaction, at least three per lease and at most half the membership window apart (today every second), busy or idle: it sets `SeenAt` and `LeaseUntil` and reads the partitions this process owns with their epochs; the membership reads run after it, outside any transaction holding a node record. The Worker stands down every partition it held when it sent the heartbeat that is missing from that answer or at another epoch, as after a refused fence; a partition taken after the send waits for the next answer. The answer is reconciled the other way too: a partition listed as this process's at an epoch the Worker does not hold, with no take of it in flight between the heartbeat's send and its answer, no retake in progress and no other Worker of the host holding it (a host with several Workers divides the answer, each judging the partitions it holds), is released at that epoch, or taken back, before the next heartbeat, so a lost release or retake answer cannot strand a row with a live process (the per-partition lease used to expire it). An answer is judged only within the Worker generation it was sent in; a Worker that reset in between discards it. When a partition's backlog estimate crosses a mark and it has had no commit for one heartbeat interval, its owner writes the estimate in a fenced commit with no journal rows ([fairness](fairness-and-backpressure.md)). A take reads the owners' node records with a plain read first, holding nothing, then writes its own node record, then locks partition rows in ascending id; a heartbeat writes its own node record, then reads partition rows; a commit locks only partition rows; the take's transaction runs at read committed on every engine, whatever the store's configured isolation. | Coordinator design choice; [ownership and fencing](ownership-and-fencing.md#the-nodes-heartbeat) |
| 19 Nov | The commit fence is unchanged except that it renews no lease. A batch with no journal rows still checks and locks every touched row at its epoch and position: the owned SQL sends the unchanged update, and the EF reference path marks `Position` modified even when unchanged. On MongoDB the fence writes each touched partition document (it increments `fenced`), so a concurrent take conflicts with the commit. | Coordinator design choice; [ownership and fencing](ownership-and-fencing.md#the-commit-fence) |
| 19 Nov | The local lease guard keeps its rules with one deadline per process: the latest send time plus the lease over the successful heartbeats and takes whose reply arrived before their own send time plus the lease, using the monotonic time just before each was sent. A late reply restarts nothing and never moves the deadline earlier. The rule applies within the current generation: a reply that arrives after a guard expiry belongs to the reset generation, which the generation latch drops, and a fresh take starts new ownership. An expired process stands down every partition it holds ([LEASE.md](../../prototypes/kernel-model/LEASE.md)). | Coordinator design choice |
| 19 Nov | Release and the operator's forced take keep their rules. A host renews its node record only while each of its Workers that holds a partition has completed a pass within the last lease (a Worker that never held a partition, or released its partitions, is live; one that stood down because its guard expired keeps those partitions pending: they count as held for the gate, the reconciliation never releases them, and it retakes them at new epochs counting a death for each one's previous-epoch trial messages, a gain or set take that includes one being its retake; or, while its passes do not complete, its lease expires and another process's take counts them. A pending entry ends when its retake lands or is refused, or an answer lists the partition at another owner or epoch; a pass completes without the pending partitions before the heartbeat and the retake, and the host keeps passing a Worker whose only partitions are pending), so a Worker whose commit, poll or load hangs loses its lease and the takeover counts the death the trial protocol needs, as the per-partition beat did (Astra review NL-M2). A host that withholds its heartbeat takes nothing: no gain, retake after a reset or activation; every host wakes each Worker for a pass at least once per poll interval, on a loop apart from the balancing round. A heartbeat carries a timeout of a third of the lease, and a take's plain node read and its own-record write together carry one such timeout (the take's `LeaseUntil` being the time taken in that write), enforced by the server on PostgreSQL and MongoDB and by the client's timer on SQL Server and MySQL (K4 qualifies it), so the lease expires within two and a third leases of the last completed pass, and the takeover follows within one lock bound and one balancing round. The store rolls back instead of committing once the lease has passed since its batch was sent; the server ends a gone client's transaction, and a host's watchdog closes the connection of an owner-path store call that outlasts twice the lease, ending the process only when the connection cannot be closed (the application's policy in the Embedded profile; the SQL Server bound); a batch's command timeout is at most the lease; a take waits at most its lock timeout, no longer than the lease, then rolls back and retries (K4 qualifies these per provider). A looping turn body runs off the pass and stays with the forced take or a model-level timeout, as before. | Coordinator design choice; [observability](observability.md) |
