# Local lease deadline and delivery history (#4362)

The owner chose a local deadline before starting new work and an observed-delivery-history interpretation for `Redelivered`. The 10-second default, the database fence, and park/subscription behavior are unchanged. The deadline is conservative: the worker records its monotonic time immediately before a successful take or renewing commit and allows new turns or job bodies only until that time plus the lease. An acknowledgement delayed beyond that deadline cannot restart the clock. An expired worker drops its memory and stands down, so the host can recover durable tickets under a fresh take. A scheduled job checks both deadline and generation immediately before its body starts. An off-turn expiry latches its generation, so even a delayed successful renewal reply cannot revive it; only the owner loop resets the worker.

The check assumes the supplied monotonic clock advances at the rate used to bound the lease. It requires no additional store operation. It cannot stop an already-running effect, nor prove that a local clock estimate matches the database's lease in the presence of clock skew or a racing takeover. The fence still decides whose commit lands. A job or activity with outside effects can run more than once and needs idempotency or reconciliation where repetition matters.

`Redelivered` reports whether the row, when polled, already had a seen or trial marker. It does not prove execution; false does not prove that another owner did not execute the message. For an External activity, the simulation's N6 compares the turn's combined hint with the combined original inbox markers, independently of later marking; it does not distinguish individual messages when a turn has several. The old check asserted every repeated external effect must have been flagged; the owner did not adopt that stronger guarantee. Historical counts in FINDINGS remain observations of those runs, not a promise.

The deterministic `--lease-check` uses real workers and the in-memory store. It delays successful take, seen-mark and ticket-commit replies past the local deadline; confirms no new stale work begins; takes over and drains the durable work; and lets an already-started noncooperative external effect finish after expiry. Two overlap tests hold a renewal acknowledgement after it lands, then either decline a queued job or discard a completed result after the old deadline: the late renewal must not keep the ticket stranded, and a replacement drains it. Both tests failed before the expiry latch. A queued same-worker retake test confirms cancellation. A separate witness pauses an already-entered scheduler delegate immediately before its guard, disposes and retakes the same worker, and then releases it; disabling the generation check makes that witness fail. `--lease-check --flaw IgnoreLeaseDeadline` must fail. `--native --flaw HideDeliveryHistory` must trip N6. No extra database round trip is introduced by the guard.

## Simulation settling schedule

The former fault-free settling loop advanced the clock by 11 seconds on every round and gave each owner eight passes. With a guard that stands down after 10 seconds, that loop itself expires every healthy owner before its next passes. The first 2,000×1,000 native sweep failed IDLE at seed 1084: each round retook the partition and polled the first batch again before it could drain. Changing every later increment to one second exposed seed 140: three deliberately fatal messages required repeated real owner deaths and lease expirations, exhausting 150 one-second rounds. Settling now advances 11 seconds when a partition has no local owner (and on the first round), otherwise one second; it retains the 150-round idle check, poison handling and all existing invariant checks. The original mutation suites must still catch their flaws. These schedules differ from prior runs, so counts and seeds are not directly comparable.

## Local checks

On this branch, the deterministic witness passed all 22 checks; disabling the lease guard failed its delayed take, mark, ticket and scheduler-dispatch checks, and disabling the generation check failed the entered-callback witness. Suppressing the history hint made N6 fail in 92 of 1,000 native seeds of 300 actions (first seed 14). With the revised settling schedule, both the general and native 20,000-seed × 1,000-action sweeps had zero failed seeds; they parked 11 and 20 innocent instances respectively. The native run compared 386,472 finished instances and saw 779 repeated effects; all 779 happened to carry the hint in that run. This result does not change the hint's contract.

The large sweeps ran on the final worker implementation; afterwards only the deterministic witness gained its two timely-renewal checks. The existing 39 kernel mutation entries and all 9 native/graph mutations were caught within their 20,000-seed caps. Six kernel mutation entries were caught by the 150-round IDLE result rather than a dedicated safety invariant, so a matching count alone does not establish unchanged detection strength after the settling change. The native smoke passed all 34 checks. SQLite native cost output for both fences was byte-identical to base `6854e81dcc` (commands, commits, round trips and state sizes). PostgreSQL cost, the 200,000-seed 300-action sweep, and #4353's distinct generator and integration were not run here. The deterministic checks use an in-memory store, not a production provider.
