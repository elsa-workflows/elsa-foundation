Four, recorded at `212f2ae6e` (review round 4), cover the driver closing its connection when a drain fails partway, and the preparer stopping a drain at the snapshot bound, within the retained-input budget and counting the index in it; the snapshot-bound entry supersedes round 1's `preparer-line-bound`, whose code the retained-input bound rewrote. Two, recorded at `b1d81e2e3` (review round 3), cover the driver's maximum and a 540 reply (`basic.get` on a stream queue) being a rejection. Six, recorded at `9411620cf` (review round 2, one superseded), cover the writer turning a blank body and a multi-line body that is not JSON into a rejected line, the execution acknowledging at all, the driver's drain time bound and its taking at least one message, and an acknowledgement-timeout 406 being transient; together with round 1's entries (and, for `writer-line-breaks`, round 6's) they supersede three entries recorded at `9b4c621db` (`writer-line-breaks`, `execution-acks` and `driver-first-too-large`), whose code the round-1 streaming rewrite (`7852daed4`) replaced; the first-message bound the last of them covered moved from the driver to the preparer, where round 1's `preparer-first-too-large` covers it. Seven, recorded at `7852daed4` (review round 1, one superseded), cover the execution disposing the receipt and acknowledging with the caller's token, the preparer choosing transient or rejected, failing a first message over the bound, and checking the bound on each written line, and the writer dropping a byte order mark and turning line breaks into spaces. The other 17, recorded at `9b4c621db` (five superseded), cover the compiler's queue and maximum rules; the worker's queue and maximum re-checks; the binding's allowed queue, its need for the consumer and the queues in its handle; the snapshot writer's line-break and blank-body handling; the per-attempt snapshot identity; the preparer requeuing on failure; the execution acknowledging at all and only after the record is applied or replayed; the JSON Lines processing view; and, on the real broker, the driver's manual acknowledgement, its multiple acknowledgement and its first-message bound.

**Not bite-proven or not tested:**
- **The crash window after the record and before the acknowledgement.** Its outcome, the broker redelivering the messages to a later run, follows from the broker's own semantics, which the container suite proves for an unacknowledged receipt; no test kills a worker inside that window.
- **A worker process killed before the record.** Equivalent to a receipt closed unacknowledged, which the container suite proves on the broker and the journeys prove through the runtime; no test kills a worker process holding a real broker connection.
- **Guards tested but not bite-proven:** the driver rejecting a message that does not fit back to the queue (closing the channel would requeue it as well); the batch builder's, record codec's and runtime API's acceptance of a `RabbitMq` source pin; the acknowledgement failure being logged rather than failing the run; the reader's empty-queue-name bound and the compiler's and reader's lower bound on the maximum; the row transformer's and connector bindings' admission of a RabbitMQ source; the acknowledgement-timeout 406's two reply-text matches, each on its own; and the Studio controls.
- **Guards neither tested nor bite-proven:** the reader's 255-character queue-name upper bound; the compiler's binding-alias check for the source; the preparer re-resolving the pinned binding revision, handle and allowed queue at preparation; the driver's stop after a refused body (removing it makes the drain refetch the requeued body forever rather than fail a test); the preparer letting the caller's cancellation, and its own preparation and binding failures, pass through without classifying them as broker failures; the execution abandoning an acknowledgement when the host stops; and the driver sending no acknowledgement for a receipt that took no message.
- **The lookup's share of the retained-input budget:** the lookup is published before the drain and its bytes are taken from the budget; no RabbitMQ source test declares a lookup.
- **Untested:** a failure writing the snapshot itself being reported as a snapshot write failure rather than classified as a broker failure; more than one consumer being registered.
- **Defensive re-checks, tested through the paths that use them but not bite-proven:** the preparer's re-checks of a RabbitMQ source pin (no digest, a hashed handle) and of the source's fields, queue and maximum; the executable reader's field-count bound and JSON pointer validation for the source; the artifact codec refusing null field entries; the compiler's size budget for the source; the connector bindings' ambiguity check for a source alias; the record codec's and preparer's refusal of HTTP shards on a RabbitMQ source; the preparer's `etl.rabbitmq-unavailable` fallbacks for missing bindings or consumer; and the executable reader requiring the source's binding alias.

## Actual Workbench journey

[`rabbitmq_source_workbench_journey.py`](rabbitmq_source_workbench_journey.py) starts its own RabbitMQ 4.1 container and its own Workbench on ephemeral loopback ports and publishes five messages, one not JSON. A RabbitMQ → JSON Lines definition with a maximum of three messages runs twice from native Flowcharts:
- **first:** takes three messages, delivers Ada and Bram and routes the invalid body to errors, through the BusinessError port; two messages stay queued and none is left unacknowledged;
- **second:** takes Cleo and Dee through the Done port; the queue is then empty with nothing unacknowledged.

All eight checks pass. The summary is [`workbench-journey-summary.json`](workbench-journey-summary.json), recorded at `2327e565f` with a clean tree. Earlier runs at `d747a18c9`, `7852daed4`, `9411620cf`, `b1d81e2e3`, `212f2ae6e` and `b42c7547f` passed the same checks. Queue counts are read live with `rabbitmqctl`; the management API's statistics lag by seconds.

## Limits

These are stated in the ETL README:
- **At least once:** messages whose acknowledgement does not reach the broker are read again by a later run; records carry no message id.
- **Finite:** a run drains what is queued, up to its maximum; a continuous source is deferred.
- **Two runs reading one queue** take different messages.
